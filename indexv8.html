How this works, and what it can’t tell you

What is standing. The laser looks along each line for something to m high, as in v7. The survey came with every point already sorted by the surveyor: ground, plants, building, or “unclassified” for anything else. A fence or wall lands in unclassified and a hedge in plants. So: if % or more of the points standing on the line are unclassified, it is a fence or wall. Some, but less, and we can’t tell: often a fence with plants over it. None, and it is hedge or plants. Nothing along most of it is nothing standing. Hidden under trees or roofs, or fewer than points, is can’t tell.

How sure. Leaves reflect near infrared strongly. Council’s 2024-25 colour infrared photo gives each point a green reading, and anything over counts as leafy: halfway between the survey’s roofs and its trees. When the photo agrees with the laser (a fence with a bare top, a hedge that reads leafy) we say high confidence, otherwise medium. Can’t tell is always low.

What it would do. From the flow test in v7: a solid m fence on the line either moves the flow path (divert), holds water back (block), or lets the water past (negligible). On hedge or plants we say uncertain, because a hedge lets some water through and we don’t know how much. Nothing standing is negligible.

How good is it. We chose the cut-offs with those checks in view, so they are not yet a fair test: the next 40 in check.html are. On lines m off every boundary, where no fence should be, it finds a fence or wall . Some of that is sheds, cars and garden walls, which the brief counts too.

What it can’t tell you. Whether a fence is solid or has gaps, so every record says permeability unknown. A fence buried in a hedge reads as plants. The line is the LINZ legal boundary, not where the fence stands; the laser’s best fit sits m off it on average. Height is the laser’s median top, plants and all. Nothing has been checked on the ground, and none of this is authoritative.
