    What this assumes

    Nothing here is detected. These are the places where a fence would change the outcome if there is one, which is where to look first.

    A fence means the whole boundary two properties share, solid and m tall. One at a time: every other boundary stays open. We tested each boundary that a flow path draining 2,000 m² or more crosses on open ground, and left out shared boundaries longer than 200 m.

    A fence moves a flow path when 10 m or more of ground gains at least 2,000 m² of catchment, more than 5 m from any flow path that was there before. It puts a property on a flow path when 3 m or more of that new route crosses a property that had none. A property is one land parcel, so a school or a block of flats can count as several.

    The model has no volumes or time. Water keeps coming until it finds a way out, however deep that gets, and the fence never leaks or falls over. The depth says how much water the fence would have to hold. Fences that need more than m rank below the ones that don't.

    Buildings are solid blocks 3 m tall. No kerbs or stormwater pipes. Catchment sizes only count land inside this map.
