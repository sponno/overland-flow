How it works, and what it gets wrong

Where it's drawn. The laser scan fits a line through whatever stands within 1.5 m of the boundary, and draws each metre of it where something 0.5 to 2.6 m high stands. Where the laser saw bare ground and nothing on it, the line is open: a driveway, a gate, a path. Breaks under 2 m are bridged, and bits under 2 m standing alone, like a letterbox, are left out.

Fence, hedge, can't tell. The same model as v10. Each boundary is cut into 6 m pieces and each piece gets a chance of being built, from three things: how much of the laser survey's standing points it left unclassified, how thin the standing thing is, and how sharp a straight edge the photo shows. A boundary is a fence where any piece reaches 80%.

Retaining wall. Where the bare ground drops 0.5 m or more across the boundary, beyond the slope either side, drawn on the legal line along the stretch that steps, for boundaries that step along 6 m or more. Read from the 1 m bare-earth model, so a low wall or one set back from the boundary is missed. People often record a fence on top of a retaining wall as a fence, so the checks can't fully score this.

Fitted on AI-made labels. The model learned from labels Claude made from the evidence pictures, unchecked. Every line a person checked was left out of the fit, so the checks score it fairly.

Known misses. A fence behind or under a hedge reads as plants. A wire fence barely shows in the laser. Cars on a driveway read as built. Fence and wall can't be told apart, nor solid from see-through.

Lines. LINZ legal boundaries, not surveyed fences. A fence more than 1.5 m off its boundary, or not on a boundary at all, isn't looked for.
